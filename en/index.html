A soprano with a scientist’s eye

Understand the movement before you sing the note

Carmen was born in Cumaná (Venezuela) and holds degrees in Education and Biomedical Science. That scientific training gave her a question that has guided her whole career: what exactly happens in the body when a note sounds free, and when it sounds tight?

Her answer is a concrete method. It means understanding abdominal breathing, jaw protrusion and the work of the lips until the sound is placed on the vomer. Then each interval in the vocalise is applied to those same intervals in the pieces. The student gains independence and stops relying on imitation.

Time invested in learning vocal technique is time gained in studying the repertoire.Carmen Moreno

The method

Four movements for a free voice

Carmen’s method is not about imagining colours or reaching “up” for high notes. It is based on anatomical movements that can be learned, observed and corrected.

01 · Air

Abdominal breathing

The rib and abdominal muscles create the support pressure that sustains long phrases, held notes and high notes.

02 · Jaw

Protrusion

The jaw moves forward diagonally, without shifting to one side, to free the high note before it arrives.

03 · Lips

Lip corners close together

Corners close together, lower lip dropping and upper lip rising to bring the harmonics closer and give the sound its ring.

04 · Sound

Placement on the vomer

The final goal of the vocalise: a resonant, free and bright sound, in the same place in the face for low and high notes.

The book

Mi técnica vocal paso a paso

Everything a singer wants to know (in Spanish): how to breathe, how to let the jaw drop, where to place the sound and what role the lips and their corners play.

It was launched on Sant Jordi’s Day 2012 in Barcelona. It includes a 3D DVD showing what happens inside the abdominal cavity and the face while you sing.
